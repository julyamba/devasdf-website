1. Who we are

Devasdf ("Devasdf", "we", "us" or "our") is an independent software startup that develops mobile apps and games. We are the developer and data controller for the apps listed in this policy.

2. What this policy covers

This policy explains what information our apps and our website (devasdf.vercel.app) collect, how it is used, and the choices you have. It applies to:

  • Dejadot (Android, package com.dejadot.app)
  • Helpline (Android, package com.helpline.app)
  • Helpline Track (Android, package com.helpline.track), used by response stations
  • WayBack (Android, package com.wayback.app)

Each app has its own section below. If an app is not listed, this policy does not cover it.

3. Dejadotcom.dejadot.app · Game

Information we collect

We do not collect any personal information through Dejadot. The game has no sign-up, no ads, no analytics and no crash-reporting service, and it does not send your gameplay data to us.

Information stored on your device

To play, the app saves the following only on your phone: scores, best runs, stats, achievements and streaks; unlocked themes and progress; settings such as sound, haptics and steering sensitivity; and the profile name and look you choose in the app.

Permissions

  • Vibration: for haptic feedback during play. You can turn haptics off in Settings.

Backups and sharing

Android may back up app data to your Google account (Android Auto Backup); this is controlled by you in your phone's settings and handled by Google. If you turn on Progress backup, the app writes a file to a location you choose; we never receive it. When you tap Share, the app creates an image or video on your phone and opens Android's share menu. Nothing is sent until you choose an app to share with.

Google Play Games

If online leaderboards through Google Play Games are available and you choose to use them, your scores and Play Games profile name are handled by Google under the Google Privacy Policy.

4. Helplinecom.helpline.app · Emergency directory & SOS

Helpline is an emergency contact directory with an SOS feature. To show the emergency numbers closest to you, it needs to know where you are. If you choose to send an SOS, it shares your details with nearby response stations so they can reach you.

Information we collect and why

DataWhy we use itWhere it goes
Precise and approximate location (latitude and longitude) To find and list emergency contacts near you. Sent over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) to our Helpline server (helplineapp.xyz) with each nearby-contacts lookup. It is not linked to your name or an account, and it is not used for advertising, profiling or sold to anyone.
Location coordinates for place names To show a readable place name (for example a city or street) for your current position. Sent to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service. See Third-party services.
Location history and recent calls So you can quickly return to places and numbers you used before. Stored only on your device. Not sent to us.
Search history To search the directory and show your recent searches. Stored only on your device.
Directory entry IDs of emergency contacts in your history To check whether those emergency numbers have been updated, so you never call an outdated number. Sent to our Helpline server. These are IDs of entries in our directory. Helpline does not read or upload the contacts in your phone's address book.

SOS (Help tab)

The SOS feature is optional. Nothing below is sent unless you fill in your Help profile and tap to send an SOS.

DataWhy we use itWhere it goes
Help profile: full name, optional profile photo, mobile and/or telephone number, address So responders know who needs help, can recognise you, call you and find you. Saved on your device. A copy is sent with each SOS you send.
Precise location and its accuracy So responders can see where you are and route to you. Sent with each SOS.
Note you write with the SOS To describe the emergency. Sent with the SOS.
Text and voice messages between you and responders So you and the responder can talk while help is on the way. Stored with the SOS request. Voice notes are stored as audio files under random, unguessable web addresses so both sides can play them.
Random device ID created by the app So only your phone can see, reply to and cancel your own requests. Sent with each SOS. It is not your phone's hardware ID or advertising ID.

Who can see your SOS: only response stations signed in to Helpline Track whose alert area includes your location, and Devasdf for operating and securing the service. They use it only to respond to your emergency. A responder's reply may show you their station name, email and address.

Where it is stored: SOS data is stored with our backend provider, Supabase, over an encrypted connection.

How long: SOS requests, messages, voice notes and photos are deleted 30 days after the request is resolved, cancelled or expires. You can cancel an active SOS at any time in the app.

Important: the SOS feature depends on a nearby station being available and online. Always call your local emergency number directly if you are in danger.

Permissions

  • Location (precise and approximate): used only while you use the app, to find nearby emergency contacts. You can deny it and still browse and search the directory.
  • Internet / network state: to download contacts, check for directory updates and send SOS requests.
  • Microphone: only to record a voice message when you press record in an SOS conversation.
  • Camera or photos (through the system picker): only when you choose a profile photo for your Help profile.

Phone calls

When you tap a number, Helpline opens your phone's dialer with the number filled in. The call is made by your phone; Helpline does not record calls or their content.

No account, no ads

Helpline does not require an account, and it does not contain ads or analytics SDKs. To check whether you are online, the app may briefly contact a Google server; no personal data is sent.

5. Helpline Trackcom.helpline.track · Responder console

Helpline Track is used by response stations (for example rescue, medical or security teams) to receive and answer SOS requests sent from Helpline. It is not a public app; station accounts are created by Devasdf.

Information we collect about stations

  • Account: the station's email address and password, handled by our sign-in provider (Supabase Auth). Passwords are stored only in hashed form.
  • Station settings: station name, alert radius, sound preference and location mode, saved on our server so they follow the station to any device.
  • Station location: from the device's GPS or set by hand. Used to decide which SOS requests the station can see and to draw routes.
  • Responses: text and voice messages the station sends, plus the station name, email and address attached to a response. These are shown to the person who asked for help.

Information stations receive

Stations see the SOS details described in the Helpline SOS section, but only for requests inside their alert area and only while they are active. Stations must use this information only to respond to the emergency, and must not copy, share or keep it for any other purpose.

Permissions

  • Location (precise and approximate): to place the station on the map and route to people who need help.
  • Microphone: only to record a voice reply when the responder presses record.
  • Internet: to receive SOS requests in real time.

Map and routing services

Map images come from OpenStreetMap, place search uses OpenStreetMap Nominatim, and driving routes are calculated by the OSRM routing service. To draw a route, the station's position and the requester's position are sent to OSRM. See Third-party services.

6. WayBackcom.wayback.app · Offline navigation

WayBack records the route you travel and guides you back along it. It works fully offline and does not send any of your data to us or anyone else. It does not request the internet permission for normal operation.

Information stored on your device

  • Recorded trails: location points, distance, time, travel mode and height changes, saved as local files in the app's storage.
  • Marked spots: notes you write and photos you take with your camera app, saved inside the app.
  • Sensor logs (only if you turn on Sensor log in Settings): raw motion, compass and pressure readings from your walks, saved on your device.
  • Settings, such as theme and preferences.

Permissions

  • Precise and approximate location: to record your route and guide you back.
  • Location in the background (foreground service): so recording continues while the screen is off or you switch apps. A persistent notification is always shown while recording, and recording stops when you end it.
  • Notifications: to show that ongoing recording notification.
  • Wake lock: to keep recording reliably while the screen is off.
  • Motion, compass and pressure sensors: for step counting, direction and height when GPS is unavailable. These sensors need no special permission.
  • Camera (through your camera app): only when you choose to add a photo to a marked spot.

Sharing and copies you control

If you tap Share sensor logs, the app creates a zip file and opens Android's share menu; nothing is sent until you choose where to send it. If you choose a folder under Keep trails after uninstall, WayBack copies your trails and photos to that folder on your device so you can restore them later.

9. Security

Data that our apps send over the network is encrypted in transit with HTTPS. Data stored on your device is protected by Android's app sandbox, so other apps cannot read it. No method of transmission or storage is 100% secure, but we work to protect your information and keep collection to a minimum.

10. Data retention

  • On-device data (all apps): kept until you delete it in the app, clear the app's storage, or uninstall the app.
  • Helpline location lookups: used to answer the request. Any server logs containing them are kept for no longer than 30 days for security and troubleshooting, then deleted.
  • Helpline SOS requests, messages, voice notes and photos: deleted 30 days after the request is resolved, cancelled or expires.
  • Helpline Track station accounts and settings: kept while the station account is active, and deleted within 30 days after the account is closed.
  • Emails you send us: kept as long as needed to help you, and then deleted on request.

11. Data deletion

You can delete your data at any time:

  • Data on your phone: delete items in the app, or go to Settings → Apps → [app name] → Storage → Clear storage, or uninstall the app. For WayBack, also delete the WayBack folder if you chose to keep copies of your trails.
  • An active SOS: cancel it in the Help tab. It is then deleted 30 days later, or sooner if you ask us.
  • Data on our servers: email us at jyamz7690@gmail.com with the subject "Data deletion request" and the app name. We will confirm and complete the request within 30 days.

Dejadot, Helpline and WayBack do not require an account. Helpline Track stations can ask us to close their account and delete its data by emailing us.
